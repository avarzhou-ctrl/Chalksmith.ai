Light: Reflection and Refraction

The Physics of Light Propagation and Boundary Interactions

The Nature of Light

  • Electromagnetic Radiation: Light is a form of energy that behaves both as a wave and as a stream of particles (photons).
  • Speed of Light ($c$): In a vacuum, light travels at its maximum possible speed:
    $c \approx 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$
  • Light travels in straight lines (rectilinear propagation) until it encounters a boundary between different media.

Reflection of Light

When light strikes a polished or reflective boundary, it bounces back into the same medium. This phenomenon is called reflection.

The Laws of Reflection:

  • The angle of incidence ($\theta_i$) is equal to the angle of reflection ($\theta_r$).
  • The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.

Law of Reflection Diagram

Key Parameters

Normal: Perpendicular line to the interface at the incidence point.

$\theta_i$: Angle of Incidence.

$\theta_r$: Angle of Reflection.

$\theta_i = \theta_r$

Spherical Mirrors

Concave Mirrors: Inwardly curved. Real or virtual images; can converge parallel light rays to a point (Focus).

Convex Mirrors: Outwardly curved. Only create virtual, diminished, upright images; diverges parallel rays.

Mirror Formula:
$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u}$

u: Object distance

v: Image distance

f: Focal length ($f = R/2$)

Magnification ($m$):

$m = -\frac{v}{u} = \frac{h_i}{h_o}$

Refraction of Light

When light passes obliquely from one transparent medium to another, its speed changes, causing the ray to bend at the interface. This is Refraction.

  • Moving from Rarer to Denser medium: Light bends towards the normal.
  • Moving from Denser to Rarer medium: Light bends away from the normal.

Snell's Law of Refraction

The extent of bending is determined by the Refractive Index ($n$) of the medium:

$n = \frac{c}{v}$

Where $c$ is speed of light in vacuum, and $v$ is speed of light in the medium.

Snell's Law: For a given pair of media, the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant:

$n_1 \sin \theta_1 = n_2 \sin \theta_2$

Refraction Visualization

Analysis

Since Medium 2 is optically denser ($n_2 > n_1$):

  • Light slows down in Medium 2.
  • Angle $\theta_2$ is smaller than $\theta_1$.
  • The ray bends towards the normal.

Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

Occurs when light travels from a denser to a rarer medium, and the angle of incidence exceeds a specific critical angle ($\theta_c$).

$\sin \theta_c = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$

Conditions for TIR:

  • Light must travel from optically denser to rarer medium ($n_1 > n_2$).
  • Angle of incidence inside denser medium must be greater than $\theta_c$.

Applications:

• Optical Fibers: Confining and transmitting signal data through glass cables using continuous TIR.

• Mirages: Optical illusions caused by continuous atmospheric refraction.

Glossary & Summary

Reflection
Bouncing of light rays off a surface back into the originating medium. Controlled by $\theta_i = \theta_r$.
Refraction
Bending of light as it transfers cross-medium due to phase velocity changes. Governed by Snell's Law.
Refractive Index
Dimensionless indicator ($n = c/v$) representing how slow light travels compared to vacuum.
Critical Angle
The minimum incidence angle in a denser medium where the angle of refraction is exactly $90^\circ$.

Understanding these physical properties enables modern optics, corrective lenses, and high-speed fiber communication.
