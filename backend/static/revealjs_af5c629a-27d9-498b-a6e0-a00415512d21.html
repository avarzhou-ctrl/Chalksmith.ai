Key Terminology

Trophic Level: The position an organism occupies in a food chain (e.g., producer, primary consumer).

Autotroph (Producer): Organisms that capture solar or chemical energy to synthesize their own food (organic molecules).

Heterotroph (Consumer): Organisms that must ingest other organisms to obtain energy and organic carbon nutrients.

Food Chains vs. Food Webs

Food Chain

A linear path showing how energy is transferred from one organism to another.

Grass → Rabbit → Fox

Food Web

A network of interconnected food chains representing all feeding relationships.

Realistic & highly resilient to environmental changes.

Arrows point in the direction of energy flow (from consumed to consumer).

The Energy Pyramid

An ecological pyramid graphically shows the total energy present at each trophic level.

  • Producers form the broad base (greatest biomass and energy).
  • Primary Consumers feed on producers.
  • Secondary Consumers feed on primary consumers.
  • Tertiary/Apex Consumers occupy the highest level.

The 10% Rule & Thermodynamics

On average, only about 10% of the chemical energy available at one trophic level is transferred to the next.

Where does 90% go?

  • Metabolic Heat: Cells convert energy to perform work, releasing heat (entropy).
  • Waste & Biomass: Undigested materials left for decomposers.
  • Life Activities: Movement, growth, and cellular repair.

Thermodynamics Connection

According to the Second Law of Thermodynamics, energy transfers are never 100% efficient. With every transfer, usable energy is lost as heat, limiting the height of trophic pyramids to 4 or 5 levels.

Trophic Cascades & Apex Predators

When an apex predator is removed or reintroduced, the ecological effects cascade down through every trophic level of the food web.

Top-Down Control

Predators suppress the abundance or alter the behavior of their prey, which releases the next lower trophic level from predation pressure.

Classic Example: Wolves

In Yellowstone, reintroducing gray wolves reduced elk populations, which allowed overgrazed forests to recover, restoring beaver and bird habitats.

Lesson Summary

1. Flows & Cycles

Energy enters ecosystems via solar radiation, is degraded through trophic layers, and exits as heat. Nutrients (atoms) cycle indefinitely.

2. 10% Rule limits

Each successive trophic level receives a fraction of the energy below it. This creates the characteristic pyramid structure of biomass.

3. Integrity of Webs

Loss of single keystone species can trigger massive ecosystem-wide trophic cascades, showcasing the fragility of food webs.
