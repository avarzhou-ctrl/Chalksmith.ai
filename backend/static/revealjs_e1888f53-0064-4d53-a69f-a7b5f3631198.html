Understanding Area

Measuring Two-Dimensional Space

Created by Educational Content Systems

What is Area?

  • Area is the amount of space inside the boundary of a flat (2D) object.
  • Unlike perimeter, which measures the distance around a shape, area measures the surface it covers.
  • Area is always measured in square units (e.g., cm², m², in²).

The Square Unit

To measure area, we count how many "unit squares" fit inside a shape.

A Square Unit is a square with sides of length 1.

Area of a Rectangle

Instead of counting squares, we use the dimensions:

Area = Length × Width

Note: For a square, because all sides are equal ($s$), the formula is $Area = s^2$.

Area of a Triangle

A triangle is exactly half of a rectangle with the same base and height.

Area = ½ × Base × Height

The Height must be the perpendicular distance from the base to the highest point.

Area of a Circle

Circles rely on the Radius (distance from center to edge) and the constant π (Pi ≈ 3.14159).

Area = π × r²

Compound Shapes

To find the area of complex shapes, decompose them into simpler parts.

Total Area = Area of A + Area of B

Glossary & Summary

  • Dimension: A measurable extent, such as length or width.
  • Perpendicular: Meeting at a 90-degree angle (used for height).
  • Square Unit: The standard unit for area (1 unit x 1 unit).

Remember: Always check that your units are consistent before multiplying (e.g., convert all cm to m first)!
