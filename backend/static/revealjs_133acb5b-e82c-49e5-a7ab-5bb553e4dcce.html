The 3 Types of
Variables

Designing Legitimate, Scientific Experiments

✦ Independent ✦ Dependent ✦ Controlled

What is a Variable?

In scientific research, a variable is any factor, trait, or condition that can exist in differing amounts or types.

Why they matter:

To design a valid test, you must isolate and track exactly how changes in one factor cause predictable changes in another.

1. Independent Variable

The "Cause"

The variable that is intentionally changed or manipulated by the experimenter.

  • There should only be one independent variable in a well-designed experiment.
  • Mnemonic: "I control it."

Examples:

  • The amount of liquid fertilizer given to a plant.
  • The temperature at which a chemical reaction occurs.
  • The brand of battery used in a flashlight.

2. Dependent Variable

Examples:

  • The final height of the plant.
  • The speed/rate of the reaction (gas production rate).
  • The hours the flashlight remains lit.
The "Effect"

The variable that is observed and measured. Its changes "depend" on the independent variable.

  • This represents your data collection.
  • Mnemonic: "Data collected."

3. Controlled Variables

The "Constants"

All elements that must be kept identical and unchanged throughout the experiment.

Why are they critical?

If controlled variables change, you cannot prove whether your Independent Variable or the outside factor caused your results. This creates an invalid experiment.

Plant Experiment Controls:

  • Same type of soil
  • Same amount of sunlight exposure
  • Same pot size and ambient temperature

The Experimental Ecosystem

Identify the Variables

Scenario: You are testing if the weight of a toy car affects how fast it rolls down a 10-foot ramp.

Independent Variable

Car Weight (the factor you change by adding weights).

Dependent Variable

Time to reach the bottom (the speed measured).

Controlled Variables (Constants)

The angle of the ramp, the surface material of the ramp, the release point of the car, and the type of wheels used.

Summary Cheat Sheet

Variable Type Role in Experiment Action Question to Ask
Independent The Cause Manipulate "What am I changing?"
Dependent The Effect Measure "What data am I collecting?"
Controlled The Standard Keep Same "What must not change?"
Remember: Independent affects Dependent, while Controlled protects the validity.
