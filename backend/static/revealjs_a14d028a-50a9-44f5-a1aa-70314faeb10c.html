Understanding Area

The mathematics of Surface Space

Created for Educators & Students

What is Area?

In geometry, Area is the measurement of the two-dimensional space enclosed within a boundary.

  • It is a scalar quantity.
  • Measured in square units.
  • Commonly used for floors, land, and paper sizes.

Units of Measurement

Because area measures 2D space, we use Square Units.

Examples:

  • $mm^2$ (Square Millimeters)
  • $cm^2$ (Square Centimeters)
  • $m^2$ (Square Meters)
  • $km^2$ (Square Kilometers)

Fact: To find the area, we are essentially counting how many unit squares fit inside the shape.

The Rectangle

The simplest formula for area.

Area = width $\times$ height

$A = w \cdot h$

Example:

Width = 10m

Height = 5m

Area = 50 $m^2$

The Triangle

A triangle is always half of a rectangle with the same base and height.

Area = $\frac{1}{2} \times$ base $\times$ height

$A = \frac{1}{2} (b \cdot h)$

Note: Height must be the perpendicular height, not the slant.

The Circle

Circles use the constant $\pi$ (Pi), which is approximately 3.14159.

Area = $\pi \times$ radius$^2$

$A = \pi r^2$

Key Terms:

Radius: Center to edge.

Diameter: Edge to edge through center ($d = 2r$).

If $r = 4cm$, then:
$A = \pi \times 4^2 \approx 50.27cm^2$

Composite Shapes

To find the area of complex shapes, decompose them into simpler polygons.

Total Area = A + B

  1. Split the shape.
  2. Calculate individual areas.
  3. Sum them together.

Glossary & Technical Terms

Scalar
A quantity described by a single number (magnitude) without direction.
Perpendicular
At an angle of 90° (a right angle) to a given line or surface.
Pi ($\pi$)
The mathematical constant representing the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter (~3.14159).
Polygon
A plane figure with at least three straight sides and angles.
Square Unit
The area of a square with sides of length 1. It is the standard unit of area.

Summary

✅ Area is the space inside a 2D boundary.

✅ Rectangles: $w \times h$

✅ Triangles: $\frac{1}{2} (b \times h)$

✅ Circles: $\pi r^2$

✅ Always include square units (e.g., $m^2$).
