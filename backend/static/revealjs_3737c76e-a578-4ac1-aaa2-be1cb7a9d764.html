Equivalence

Fractions, Decimals, & Percentages

Understanding how to represent parts of a whole in three distinct, interchangeable ways.

The Core Concept

Fractions, decimals, and percentages are not different values; they are different languages for expressing the exact same proportion of a whole.

Fraction

Expresses a relationship of parts to a whole using a numerator and denominator.

14

Decimal

A fractional number based on powers of ten, separated by a decimal point.

0.25

Percent

Literally translates to "per hundred" (out of 100 parts total).

25%

How to Convert

Follow these fundamental routes to convert between formats:

Fraction → Decimal

Divide the numerator by the denominator.

45 = 4 ÷ 5 = 0.8

Decimal → Percent

Multiply by 100 (shift the decimal point 2 places to the right).

0.8 × 100 = 80%

Percent → Fraction

Write the percentage over 100 and simplify.

80% = 80100 = 45

Step-by-Step: Converting Fractions

Sometimes fractions do not resolve into neat decimals. Let's observe two patterns:

Terminating Decimals

The division finishes with a remainder of zero.

38 = 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375

Ideal for clean calculations and exact percentages (37.5%).

Recurring Decimals

The digit sequence repeats infinitely.

23 = 2 ÷ 3 = 0.666... (or 0.6̄)

Typically rounded to 0.67 or written as 66.7%.

Summary Checklist

  • Fractions represent parts of a whole structurally.
  • Decimals map these relationships directly into base-10 mathematics.
  • Percentages provide a normalized benchmark of "out of 100" for fast comparison.
  • Mastery of conversions enables fluid problem-solving in science, finance, and everyday logic.
